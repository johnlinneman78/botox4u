Hi {{first_name}},

Here's something the aesthetic medicine industry doesn't advertise:

**The letters after your injector's name don't predict your results as much as you'd expect.**

Board-certified dermatologists and plastic surgeons have the most years of training. That's real. But injection technique is a specific skill — and many nurse practitioners and physician assistants who specialize in aesthetics inject more patients per week than the surgeons in town.

Volume matters. A lot.

Here's what to actually look for:

→ **How often they inject.** Someone doing 20+ Botox treatments per week has steadier hands than someone doing 5 per month — regardless of their degree.

→ **How long they've specialized in aesthetics.** A nurse practitioner with 8 years of aesthetic-only experience often outperforms a dermatologist who added Botox to their practice last year.

→ **Their complication history.** You can check your state medical board's website for any disciplinary actions. It takes 2 minutes. Do it.

The bottom line: credentials are one factor, not the only factor. That's why VerifiedAesthetics's TrueScore weights credentials at 25 out of 100 points — important, but not the whole picture.

## DAY 2: THE TOUCH-UP TRAP

**Subject:** If your provider charges for touch-ups, read this
**Preview text:** This one policy can add $200+ to your annual Botox cost

---

Hi {{first_name}},

Quick question: does your provider offer free touch-ups?

If you don't know, that's worth finding out before your next appointment. Here's why:

Botox results aren't always perfect on the first visit. Facial muscles are asymmetric. Product can diffuse slightly differently on each side. A touch-up 10-14 days after treatment is normal and expected.

**Good providers include complimentary touch-ups within 2 weeks.** They consider it part of the treatment, not an add-on.

Some providers, though, charge $50-$150 for touch-up visits. Over 3-4 sessions per year, that's an extra $200-$600 you didn't budget for.

Before your next appointment, ask this exact question:

*"If I need an adjustment after my treatment, is that included or is there an additional charge?"*

Their answer tells you two things:

1. Whether they'll cost you more than the sticker price
2. Whether they stand behind their work

Both matter.

If you get Botox regularly and aren't enrolled in these two programs, you're overpaying:

**1. Allē (by Allergan)**
This is the manufacturer's rewards program. You earn points on every Allergan treatment (Botox, Juvederm, etc.) at any participating provider. Points convert to savings on future treatments — typically $50-$75 in value per visit.

Most providers participate, but not all. Ask before you book.

Many med spas run their own points or membership programs on top of Allē. These stack — meaning you can earn rewards from both programs on the same treatment.

- Allē savings: ~$60/visit × 3 visits = **$180/year**

## DAY 4: THE ALTERNATIVES YOUR PROVIDER MIGHT NOT MENTION

**Subject:** Botox isn't the only option (and might not be the best one for you)
**Preview text:** 4 FDA-approved alternatives — and when each one makes more sense

---

Hi {{first_name}},

"Botox" has become a generic term, like "Band-Aid" or "Kleenex." But Botox is actually one of four FDA-approved neurotoxin brands — and each has differences worth knowing about.

**Botox (Allergan)** — The original. Most widely available. 3-4 month duration for most patients. Premium pricing.

**Dysport (Galderma)** — Spreads more than Botox, which can be an advantage for large areas like the forehead. Some patients report faster onset (2-3 days vs. Botox's 5-7). Often 10-15% cheaper per treatment.

**Xeomin (Merz)** — "Naked" neurotoxin with no accessory proteins. Less likely to cause resistance over time with repeated use. Similar duration to Botox. Often the most affordable option.

**Daxxify (Revance)** — The newest option. Key differentiator: lasts 6-9 months for some patients vs. 3-4 months for the others. Higher per-session cost, but potentially fewer sessions per year — which can make it cheaper annually.

Here's the thing: many providers only carry one or two brands. If yours only offers Botox, they might not mention that Dysport or Daxxify could be a better fit for you.

**The question to ask:** *"Do you offer alternatives to Botox? If so, which would you recommend for my goals and anatomy?"*

A provider who takes time to explain your options — not just default to one brand — is one who's treating you as a patient, not a revenue line.
